# BlockForge

A single-player voxel first-person shooter for the browser. Blocky world, cinematic lighting, two game modes, and a full progression and ranked system. Every other player in the game is an AI competitor.

Everything is original and generated by the game's code: textures, voxel models, sounds and music. There are no image, audio or model asset files.

## Running it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm run build      # type-check + production bundle in dist/
npm run preview    # serve dist/ on http://localhost:4173
npm run test:e2e   # headless smoke test of boot, all modes and persistence
```

You need a browser with WebGL 2: current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, with hardware acceleration turned on. It runs on phones and tablets too (see **Touch screens** below).

## Modes

**Battle Royale (Ashcrown Isle).** 24 competitors ride an airship over a large handcrafted island and drop in. The island has meadows, forest, a snow-capped mountain range, desert mesas, a river delta, villages, a castle, a mine with tunnels, a lighthouse, a dam and other landmarks. Space jumps out of the airship. The camera pulls back to a third-person skydive view; hold W to dive faster. The glider opens on its own low over the ground, or earlier with a fresh tap of Space. Players loot weapons, ammo, armor, heals, grenades and materials from the ground and from supply chests. They harvest wood, stone and metal and use them to build walls, floors, ramps and blocks, and they craft at any time. Everyone lands with 30 wood, enough for a few pieces. A storm closes in over six phases, and its waiting periods get shorter once only a few players remain. The last player alive wins.

**Team Deathmatch.** 6v6 on three purpose-built arenas: *Hamlet* (sunset village), *Quarry* (noon, a terraced pit with a crane) and *Frostbite* (a snowy night outpost). You choose a loadout and respawn after each death. Spawn protection and restricted enemy spawn zones stop spawn camping. The first team to 50 kills wins, or the team ahead when the 8-minute clock runs out. Building is disabled in this mode.

**Basics (first run).** The first visit goes straight into a one-minute, hands-on tutorial on the Training Grounds: walk to the marker, jump the wall, grab the rifle, shoot a target, build a wall. One card at the top shows the current step and its keys (or touch buttons), and a marker on screen points at the goal. Each step ticks off as soon as it is done. At the end a single card offers the first Battle Royale. Basics can be skipped at any point and replayed from the "How to play" (i) button at the top of the menu.

**Training Grounds.** An interactive tutorial with 14 steps: movement, looting, shooting, reloading, aiming, chests, sniper scopes, harvesting, building, crafting, grenades and a final duel. The steps form a checklist: the panel suggests the next one, but any step you complete counts, in any order. Downing one target dummy completes the shooting step. Press **N** (or tap **Skip step**) to move past a step.

Both competitive modes have **Casual** and **Ranked** playlists.

### Touch screens

On phones and tablets the game switches to on-screen controls (Settings → Controls → Touch controls: Auto, On or Off). Touch anywhere on the left side for a movement stick (push it to the edge to sprint) and drag on the right side to look. The fire button can also be dragged to aim while shooting. Aim and crouch are toggles. The hotbar is tappable, including the pick. The build button opens the build panel, which takes the hotbar's place; the fire button then places pieces. Pause, map and inventory (scoreboard in Team Deathmatch) sit next to the minimap, and panels close with their × button or a tap outside. Phones play in landscape. On the first run, phones and tablets start on lighter graphics settings.

## Features

- **Rendering:** a custom HDR pipeline built on three.js. It has greedy-meshed voxel chunks with per-vertex ambient occlusion and smooth sky and block light, two-cascade soft shadow maps, screen-space reflections on water (with depth tint, ripples and shoreline foam), volumetric god rays, bloom, ACES tonemapping with colour grading, and FXAA. The sky, clouds, stars and moon are procedural, and cloud shadows move across the ground. Day/night lighting changes with the sun's height. Weather includes rain, storms with lightning and snow. Foliage moves in the wind.
- **Weapons:** 8 weapons built from voxels: pistol, SMG, two assault rifles, pump shotgun, DMR, bolt-action sniper and rocket launcher. Each has its own recoil pattern, spread, damage falloff and reload animation (empty and tactical reloads). Every gun has muzzle flash, ejected shells and impact effects. Bullets are hitscan, while sniper rounds and rockets are physical projectiles. Aiming zooms in on every gun: red-dot sights centre their dot behind see-through glass, iron sights sit just below a small centre dot, and scopes magnify 2.5× to 8× with their own sensitivity. Frag and smoke grenades follow an arc that you preview before throwing.
- **AI competitors:** bots perceive, navigate (A* over the voxel grid), loot, rotate with the storm, take cover, heal, build, throw grenades and push or hold lanes. Each has an archetype, a skill level and a reaction time. Their aim errors are human-like rather than perfect.
- **Building and crafting:** a build panel shows what each piece is for (wall: cover, floor: bridge, ramp: climb, block: fill), what it costs and how much of each material you have. The first time you open it, a short card explains why and how to build, and until you have built a few pieces the game suggests building when you land and when you come under fire. Pieces are grid-snapped in wood, stone or metal with placement previews and collision checks. Each block has HP, and structures can be damaged and collapse. The world cannot be dug or tunnelled, which stops players sealing themselves in. Recipes cover ammo, heals, armor plates, grenades and material conversion.
- **Progression:** XP and levels with reward reveals, coins, a 7-day daily reward track (an epic outfit on day 2, a legendary camo on day 3, a legendary outfit on day 7), three daily quests, achievements, and earnable cosmetics (weapon camos, outfits, banners, titles). Cosmetics never affect gameplay, and nothing can be bought with real money. In the locker you can drag the character to turn it; premium outfits add armor plates, trims, masks, helmets and capes.
- **Ranked:** 8 tiers (Flint → Forgemaster). Every tier below Forgemaster has 4 divisions (IV → I). You gain and lose RP each match, get promoted or demoted with demotion protection after a promotion, and play 8-week seasons with soft resets and rewards. Opponents get tougher as you climb: at the bottom of Flint they react slowly, miss a lot and hit you for less, and by Forgemaster they are quick, accurate and use cover and grenades well. Casual matches follow your rank too, a notch easier. The ranked ladder is made of simulated AI profiles.
- **Events:** limited-time weekend events run on the real calendar of your device. Countdowns show real time remaining and you can preview upcoming events.

## About the AI competitors

BlockForge is designed to feel like an online shooter, but there are no real players, servers, accounts or chat. Everyone in your lobby, on your team, in the kill feed and on the ladder is an AI player. Each one has a persistent name, look, play style, rank and match history, and they keep that identity across matches. A single line under the play button says that all other players are computer-controlled; players are not tagged individually. The ladder compares you only with these simulated profiles. It is not a global ranking.

## Publishing on CrazyGames

The build is ready for CrazyGames, Basic Launch and Full Launch: `npm run build`, then zip the *contents* of `dist/` (so `index.html` sits at the root of the zip): `cd dist && zip -r ../blockforge.zip .`. It is four files of about 1.3 MB, all at the root of the zip with no folders (a portal upload once lost the files in an `assets/` subfolder), with relative paths throughout.

Portals may run a game in a sandboxed iframe (an opaque "null" origin), serve it from a static host without CORS headers and add a strict Content-Security-Policy. There, module scripts, `crossorigin` stylesheets, web fonts, inline scripts and inline styles can all be blocked and the page stays blank. So the build ships one classic, deferred script and one plain stylesheet file with the Latin font subsets inlined as woff2, no `crossorigin` attributes (`vite.config.ts`), and no inline scripts or `style="…"` attributes. `index.html` shows a BLOCKFORGE preloader until the game's own loading screen takes over, and `public/loader.js` watches the start: if one of the game's files fails to load, or the game has not reached its menu after 25 seconds, it shows the current loading step and the load errors with a Reload button instead of a blank or frozen page.

`src/platform/crazygames.ts` integrates the HTML5 SDK v3. It loads the SDK only when the game runs on a CrazyGames domain (or locally with `?useLocalSdk=true`). With the SDK it:

- reports `loadingStart` / `loadingStop` around boot, and `gameplayStart` / `gameplayStop` whenever a match is actually being played: only after the player clicked **Click to play** (on touch screens play starts right away), and not in menus, pause, settings, the death screen or while an ad plays;
- calls `happytime` on a win;
- follows the portal's mute setting;
- saves all progress through the SDK's data module (see **Saving**);
- uses the CrazyGames account: a signed-in player plays under their CrazyGames name and picture (read on every start and kept in sync through the auth listener). The name cannot be edited in the game, by guests either. Guests can always play; a small **Sign in** button next to the name opens the platform's sign-in dialog, only when clicked. Guests are not listed on the season ladder. There are no other login options.

Full Launch requirements, and where the game meets them:

- **Lands in gameplay**: a first visit starts the five-step Basics tutorial straight away.
- **Pause key**: `P` pauses (Esc still releases the mouse and pauses, but on portals Esc also leaves fullscreen). There is no custom fullscreen button.
- **Consistent physics at 144 and 165 Hz**: gravity is integrated exactly (`src/game/physics.ts`), so jump height and air time are the same at any frame rate; ground movement matches within 1%.
- **Fits every screen size**: nothing cut off, overlapping or scrolling just a little, at CrazyGames' embed sizes down to 640×360 with mouse and keyboard and on phones and tablets: checked by the `fit` scenario. On small windows the mouse-and-keyboard HUD shrinks each block toward its corner, and the menu, inventory and scoreboard switch to compact layouts.
- **English**, no external links, no other ads than the SDK's, and the game runs normally with ads off or blocked.

### Ads

All ads go through the SDK (`src/ads.ts`, `src/ui/adui.ts`). The game freezes and mutes while one plays.

- **Midgame ads** are requested at the natural breaks between matches: before a match starts (Find match / Play again) and when a match ends in play (win, elimination) before its results. Never when the player leaves a match or goes back to the menu: that is navigation, not a break. The SDK decides how often one really plays (at most one every 3 minutes, none right at the start, spaced from rewarded ads) and ignores the rest. None before the Basics tutorial, and none at a Battle Royale death that already offered Redeploy (CrazyGames allows a midgame ad or a "watch to keep playing" offer at one break, not both).
- **Rewarded ads**, eleven offers that each pay out only after the ad finished: 2× XP and coins on the results screen; Redeploy after a Battle Royale elimination (once a match, drop back in over the safe zone); the Battle Royale starter kit (Ranger AR, plates, bandages, wood for the next drop); Rank Shield (the next ranked match cannot cost RP); the Lucky Wheel spin; a 200-coin supply drop every 5 minutes; 15 minutes of double XP; doubling the daily reward; an extra quest reroll; Armory items at half price; and the Ad Vault, where one ad unlocks the Gilded Phantom or Void Ronin legendary outfit or the Prismatic camo (CrazyGames does not allow stacked rewarded ads). When no rewarded ad can play (ads off, an ad blocker, three no-fills in a row) every offer is hidden for a while, so no button is ever left without effect.
- **Lucky Wheel**: a free spin every 10 minutes and another any time for a rewarded ad. Prizes are 50 to 1,000 coins, an epic camo, 15 minutes of double XP, the Lucky Ace legendary outfit, or nothing; the odds are listed under the wheel.
- **Banner**: one 320×50 banner at the bottom of the main menu, on every tab (Play, Career, Locker, Armory, Ladder), nowhere else. It sits straight on the menu background without a frame, and the menu's content ends above it, so it never covers the menu. It refreshes every 35 seconds while it is on screen and the tab is visible. A `bannerCooldown` answer retries after 5 seconds; after the SDK's per-session refresh limit (`maxRefreshReached`) the last banner stays.
- **Off CrazyGames** (own hosting, previews) the ad slots show clearly labelled placeholders, so every flow can be tried; `?ads=off` turns them off.

The text for the submission form (category, tags, description and controls) is in `docs/crazygames-submission.md`.

## Saving

Your profile, settings and the AI competitors' records go through `src/core/storage.ts`. On CrazyGames every key is stored in the SDK's data module only, which syncs it to the player's CrazyGames account (the SDK keeps guests' progress until they sign in); the game never reads or writes browser storage there. Everywhere else it is saved in `localStorage` on your device, and if neither is available the game runs from memory and says so in the menu. Daily rewards, quests and events follow your device clock. You can erase all of it under **Settings → About → Reset progress**.

## Testing

`npm run test:e2e` drives the production bundle, so run `npm run build` first. The test starts its own preview server and uses headless Chromium. It boots the game, plays fast-forwarded slices of Battle Royale, Team Deathmatch and the tutorial, finishes each match through the results screen, and checks that progress survives a reload.

`tests/scenarios/` holds longer scripted sessions that also save screenshots to `tests/screens/`. Run them with `node tests/drive.mjs tests/scenarios/<name>.mjs` against `npm run preview`:

- `brfull`: a full ranked Battle Royale, logging every elimination.
- `tdm`: a full Team Deathmatch. Set `TDM_MAP` to choose the arena.
- `tutorial`: plays all 14 training steps through the real input layer.
- `ranked`: promotion, demotion, the ladder and a season rollover.
- `menutabs`: every menu tab, settings, and the daily reward and its claim limit.
- `fit`: every screen (menu tabs, Locker categories, the wheel, settings, lobby, Battle Royale and Team Deathmatch HUDs with their panels, death screens, results, the tutorials) at one window size, reporting text that is cut off, text or HUD blocks that overlap, and scroll areas that only scroll a little. Run it at each size, e.g. `node tests/drive.mjs tests/scenarios/fit.mjs 907 510`, and with `BF_MOBILE=1` for touch; `BF_SHOTS=1` saves screenshots.
- `ads`: every ad placement with the stand-in ads: the menu banner on each tab (and that it covers nothing), the Lucky Wheel (free spin, cooldown, ad spin), the boosts, the daily double, the starter kit, Redeploy while spectating (with no banner there or on the results), and 2× rewards. Run it at 1280×720, 907×510 and with `BF_MOBILE=1` at 844×390.
- `basics`: run with `BF_FIRST_RUN=1` (the driver otherwise skips the first-visit tutorial). Plays the Basics tutorial through the real input layer, screenshots every step and the closing card, and checks it does not come back after a reload.
- `mobile`: run with `BF_MOBILE=1` and a phone or tablet size, e.g. `BF_MOBILE=1 node tests/drive.mjs tests/scenarios/mobile.mjs 844 390`. Screenshots every screen, drives the stick, look, hotbar, build bar and overlay buttons with real touch events, and reports any HUD or touch control that overlaps another or leaves the screen.
- Diagnostics: `vm` (weapon viewmodels at the hip and aiming down sights), `build` (placement onto the training ledge), `brearly` (landing spread, bot accuracy and early eliminations), `perf` (simulation cost and a CPU profile) and `mem` (geometry and memory across modes).
